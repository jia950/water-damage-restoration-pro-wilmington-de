Sewage Backup Cleanup in Wilmington, DE

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water and contains harmful bacteria and pathogens. Sewage backup cleanup should be handled by trained crews wearing protective equipment who extract the water, remove contaminated porous materials, disinfect surfaces and verify the area is safe to reoccupy.

Why Sewage Backup Cleanup Is Needed in Wilmington

Sewage backups can come from clogged lines, tree roots, heavy storms overwhelming sewers or a failed pump. Sewage is Category 3 or black water, so it can carry bacteria and viruses. Porous materials touched by it are normally removed rather than cleaned.

Signs You Need Sewage Backup Cleanup

  • Toilets or drains backing up
  • Foul odor from floor drains
  • Dark, contaminated water in basements

Our Sewage Backup Cleanup Process

  1. Keep people and pets out
  2. Extract contaminated water
  3. Remove porous materials
  4. Disinfect and deodorize, then dry

What Affects the Cost of Sewage Backup Cleanup

Cost depends on volume, the area affected, how much material must be removed and the sanitizing needed. The plumber who clears the line is a separate cost.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cleaning sewage with household cleaners and a mop
  • Letting children or pets near the area
  • Running the HVAC system with contamination present
  • Keeping soaked carpet or drywall

When to Call

Call immediately. Stop using sinks, toilets and drains, leave the area and avoid contact with the water. Our 24/7 line is (302) 648-0712.

Sewage Backup: Is It Safe to Clean Up Yourself?

No. Sewage water can carry bacteria and viruses, so cleaning it yourself puts you at risk. Keep children and pets out, avoid touching the water, do not use a household vacuum and call for trained cleanup. Porous items such as carpet and drywall that touched sewage are usually thrown away, and hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected. If the main line is blocked, a plumber needs to clear it.

Sewage Backup Cleanup FAQs

Is sewage backup dangerous?

Yes. It can carry bacteria and viruses, so avoid contact and keep children and pets away.

Who fixes the clogged line?

A plumber clears the blockage. A restoration crew handles cleanup and sanitizing.

Why does sewage have to be removed rather than cleaned?

Porous items such as carpet, padding and drywall can hold contamination that surface cleaning cannot reach.

Can I stay in the house during cleanup?

It depends on the extent. The crew will tell you whether the area can be isolated safely.

Who fixes the cause of a sewage backup?

A plumber or the sewer authority fixes a blocked or damaged line. The cleanup crew removes the contaminated water and materials, cleans and sanitizes.
