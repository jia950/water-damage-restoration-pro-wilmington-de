Flood Damage Restoration in Wilmington, DE

Wilmington sits between the Delaware River, the Christina River and the Brandywine, so storm surge and heavy rain can flood homes quickly. Flood damage restoration means removing contaminated water, discarding materials that cannot be saved, cleaning and sanitizing, and drying the structure completely before repairs begin.

Why Flood Damage Restoration Is Needed in Wilmington

Flooding in the Wilmington area can come from storm surge, nor'easters, heavy rain and overflowing creeks and rivers. Flood water is often contaminated with soil, bacteria and chemicals, so it is treated as unsafe, and porous materials that touch it usually cannot be saved.

Signs You Need Flood Damage Restoration

  • Water entering from outside after storms
  • Muddy or discolored water on floors
  • Damaged flooring, drywall and contents

Our Flood Damage Restoration Process

  1. Safety check and utility shutoff
  2. Water and debris removal
  3. Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment
  4. Structural drying and moisture verification

What Affects the Cost of Flood Damage Restoration

Flood restoration costs depend on how high the water rose, how long it stayed, how contaminated it was and how much of the structure and contents were affected.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Walking through flood water without protection
  • Turning on electronics or the furnace while wet
  • Keeping wet carpet padding or insulation
  • Starting repairs before the structure is fully dry

When to Call

Call as soon as it is safe to enter. If water is still rising or power is on, leave and call the utility and emergency services first. Our 24/7 line is (302) 648-0712.

Water Damage vs Flood Damage: What Is the Difference?

Water damage usually comes from inside the home, such as a burst pipe, a failed water heater or a leaking appliance. Flood damage comes from outside, such as heavy rain, overflowing creeks or rivers or storm surge. Flood water is usually more contaminated.

Brandywine Creek and Christina River Flooding in the Wilmington Area

Wilmington sits where the Christina River and Brandywine Creek meet, and both have a long flood history. During the remnants of Ida in September 2021 the Brandywine at Wilmington reached 23.14 feet, more than four feet above major flood stage, according to the National Weather Service as reported by Delaware LIVE. Tidal water from the Delaware River pushing up the Christina and Brandywine made that flooding worse, University of Delaware researchers found. The Christina River watershed also has 1,007 structures in the 100-year floodplain, the most in New Castle County, according to the University of Delaware Water Resources Center. Parts of the Southbridge neighborhood are prone to flooding, as Delaware Public Media has reported, and the National Weather Service flood history lists 26 floods at the Wilmington gauge since 1946 (as of early 2020).

Flood Cleanup Checklist for Delaware Homeowners

  • Stay out until it is safe, and keep power off in flooded areas
  • Wear gloves, boots and a mask, and keep children and pets away
  • Photograph everything before you move or throw anything away
  • Remove wet carpet, padding and soaked drywall quickly, because mold can start within 24 to 48 hours
  • Throw away porous items that touched flood water, and clean hard surfaces
  • Run dehumidifiers and fans, then have moisture readings checked

Flood Damage Restoration FAQs

Is flood water safe to touch?

No. Flood water can carry bacteria and contaminants. Avoid contact and wear protective gear.

What gets thrown away after a flood?

Porous materials such as carpet padding and wet insulation are usually removed. A technician can assess the rest.

Can flooded drywall be saved?

Usually not if it was in contact with flood water. It is typically cut out above the water line and replaced after drying.

How long after a flood can mold appear?

Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours in wet, warm conditions, which is why fast drying matters.

What is the first thing to do after a flood in my house?

Make sure everyone is safe, keep away from electrical hazards and standing water, take photos, and call for water extraction. Fast removal limits mold and structural damage.
