Water Heater Leaking? Steps to Take Right Away

Water heater leaking and flooding your Wilmington home? Do not wait. Call our 24/7 emergency water removal team now at (302) 648-0712.

Published October 29, 2026 · 4 min read

A leaking water heater can put a lot of water on the floor in a short time, and the damage often spreads into a finished basement or utility room before anyone notices. Whether it is a slow drip or a full tank failure, acting fast and in the right order keeps a water heater leak from turning into a much larger repair.

Steps to Take Right Away

  1. Turn off power to the heater at the breaker, or shut off the gas supply for a gas unit.
  2. Close the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater.
  3. Stay out of the water if outlets or the electrical panel are nearby.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area and take photos for your records.
  5. Call a plumber for the heater and a crew for emergency water removal and drying.

Common Causes of a Leaking Water Heater

  • Corrosion in an older tank, which weakens the metal over time
  • Loose or worn fittings and supply connections at the top of the unit
  • A faulty temperature and pressure relief valve that lets water escape
  • Sediment build-up at the bottom of the tank that stresses the metal
  • A cracked or failing tank, which usually means replacement rather than repair

Many tank water heaters last roughly 8 to 12 years, so a leak from an older unit often means it is time to replace it rather than patch it. A plumber can tell you quickly whether the leak is a fitting issue or a tank failure.

Why Drying Matters So Much

Even a small leak can soak carpet padding, drywall and the base of walls, and water heaters are often tucked into closets, basements or utility rooms where leaks go unnoticed for hours or days. Quick water removal and proper structural drying prevent mold and stop ongoing damage to flooring, cabinets and framing nearby. If the leak reached a finished basement, pairing basement flood cleanup with structural drying gives the best chance of saving flooring and drywall instead of replacing it.

Preventing Future Water Heater Leaks

Check your water heater every few months for rust, moisture at the base, or a puddle forming nearby. Installing a drain pan with an alarm, flushing sediment from the tank yearly, and replacing the unit proactively near the end of its expected lifespan are all simple ways to avoid an unexpected flood.

Tank vs. Tankless: Does the Type Matter?

Traditional tank water heaters hold 40 to 80 gallons of water at all times, which means a failure can release a large volume quickly. Tankless units heat water on demand and hold far less water in the system, so a leak is usually smaller, though the fittings and connections can still fail. Either type benefits from an annual inspection, and either type can cause real water damage to flooring, cabinets and nearby walls if a leak goes unnoticed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my water heater is about to fail?

Rust-colored water, popping or rumbling sounds, moisture around the base, or a water heater older than 10 years are all signs it may be time for an inspection or replacement.

Can a small water heater leak cause mold?

Yes. Even a slow, small leak can soak nearby carpet, drywall or subfloor, and mold can start within 24 to 48 hours if the area is not dried quickly.

Should I repair or replace a leaking water heater?

A loose fitting can often be repaired, but a leak from the tank itself almost always means replacement, especially on a unit over 8 to 10 years old.
