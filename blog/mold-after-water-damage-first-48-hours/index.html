Mold After Water Damage: What to Do in the First 48 Hours

Mold can grow within 48 hours of water damage. Protect your Wilmington, DE home. Call our 24/7 mold remediation team now at (302) 648-0712.

Published October 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Mold after water damage can begin to grow within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet, according to the EPA. That short window is why the first two days after a leak or flood in your Wilmington home matter so much, and why acting fast can mean the difference between a simple cleanup and a full mold remediation project.

Why Mold Grows So Fast After Water Damage

Mold spores are already present in nearly every home and in the outside air. They only need moisture, a food source such as drywall or wood, and a warm temperature to start growing. Wet carpet padding, soaked drywall and damp insulation provide exactly that environment, which is why mold after water damage can appear in as little as one to two days if materials are not dried quickly.

Your 48-Hour Plan

  1. Stop the source. Shut off the water supply or fix the leak so no more water enters the space.
  2. Remove standing water with pumps or wet vacuums as soon as it is safe to do so.
  3. Start drying. Use fans and dehumidifiers and open the space where it is safe to improve airflow.
  4. Remove what cannot dry. Wet carpet padding, soaked drywall and insulation often need to be removed rather than dried.
  5. Clean hard surfaces and keep indoor humidity below about 50 percent to slow any remaining growth.

When to Call a Mold Remediation Professional

  • The affected area is larger than about 10 square feet, the EPA guideline for what homeowners can often handle themselves
  • The water involved was sewage or flood water, which carries contamination risks
  • You smell mold but cannot see where it is, which can mean hidden growth inside walls
  • Someone in the home has breathing problems, allergies or a weakened immune system

What Professional Mold Remediation Involves

A licensed, independent contractor typically sets up containment to keep spores from spreading, removes and safely disposes of contaminated materials, and uses HEPA filtration and antimicrobial treatments to clean remaining surfaces. This is usually paired with structural drying to make sure the underlying moisture source is fully resolved, since treating visible mold without addressing the moisture almost always leads to it returning. If the mold followed a larger event like flood damage restoration or a basement flood, remediation and drying are often coordinated together.

Hidden Mold Risks You Cannot See

Mold does not always grow where you can see it. Carpet padding, the inside of walls behind baseboards, and spaces under cabinets can stay damp long after the surface looks dry. A musty smell that lingers after cleanup is often a sign that moisture, and possibly mold, is still trapped somewhere out of sight. If a smell persists more than a day or two after drying, it is worth having a professional check moisture levels inside the wall cavity rather than assuming the problem is solved.

Related Reading

The EPA publishes a helpful guide to mold and moisture in the home. You can read the EPA: A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture, and Your Home for additional cleanup details and prevention tips.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly does mold grow after water damage?

Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials, according to the EPA, which is why fast water removal and drying are so important.

Can I clean mold myself after water damage?

Small areas under about 10 square feet on hard, non-porous surfaces may be manageable with proper precautions. Larger areas or mold from sewage or flood water are best left to a professional.

Does drying alone remove mold?

Drying stops mold from spreading further, but mold that has already started growing usually needs to be physically removed and the area treated, not just dried out.
