Frozen and Burst Pipes: How to Prevent Them This Winter

Frozen or burst pipe flooding your Wilmington home? Stop the damage fast. Call our 24/7 emergency team now at (302) 648-0712 for rapid cleanup.

Published October 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Frozen and burst pipes are one of the most common causes of winter water damage in Wilmington and across New Castle County. When water freezes inside a pipe it expands, and the pressure can split the pipe wide open. The damage often shows up only after the ice melts and water starts pouring into walls, ceilings and finished basements. A few simple habits can lower the risk significantly.

Why Pipes Freeze and Burst in Delaware Winters

Delaware winters bring sharp cold snaps, and pipes in unheated or poorly insulated spaces, such as attics, crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls, are most at risk. Older homes in Wilmington with original plumbing are especially vulnerable, since copper and galvanized pipes have less flexibility than modern materials. Once ice forms inside a pipe, pressure builds between the blockage and a closed faucet, and that pressure is what causes the pipe to crack or split.

How to Prevent Frozen Pipes

  • Keep your heat on, and do not let the house get very cold, even when you are away
  • Insulate pipes in attics, crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls
  • Seal drafts around pipes where they enter the house
  • Disconnect garden hoses before freezing weather arrives
  • During extreme cold, open cabinet doors under sinks and let a faucet drip
  • Know where your main water shut-off valve is located before you need it

If a Pipe Freezes

If water stops coming from a faucet in freezing weather, a pipe may be frozen. Shut off the main valve, open the faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or heating pad. Never use an open flame, which can damage the pipe further or start a fire. If you see a crack or any leak while thawing a pipe, leave the water off and call a plumber immediately.

What to Do the Moment a Pipe Bursts

  1. Shut off the main water valve right away.
  2. Turn off electricity to wet areas if you can do it safely, and stay out of rooms with a sagging ceiling.
  3. Open a faucet to drain what is left in the lines.
  4. Move valuables out of the wet area and take photos for insurance.
  5. Call for burst pipe damage cleanup and drying so the water does not soak into walls and floors.

Why Fast Water Extraction Matters

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons of water in a short time, and that water travels fast through flooring, drywall and insulation. The longer water sits, the more materials have to be replaced instead of dried and saved. Professional emergency water removal paired with structural drying is the fastest way to limit damage and avoid mold taking hold inside your walls.

Insurance and Burst Pipe Claims

Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, though coverage can vary if the pipe froze due to a known heating failure that was not addressed. Document the damage with photos before cleanup begins, keep any receipts for emergency repairs, and ask your restoration contractor for a written scope of work to support your claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a pipe is about to burst?

Warning signs include no water from a faucet during a cold snap, strange odors near a wall, bulging pipes, or frost visible on exposed piping. Shut off the main valve if you suspect a problem.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Yes, with care. Use a hair dryer or heating pad and never an open flame. If you see any cracking or leaking, stop and call a plumber right away.

How much does a burst pipe cost to clean up?

Cost depends on how long the pipe ran, which rooms were reached and whether walls or ceilings must be opened to dry them fully. A local contractor can give you a direct estimate.
